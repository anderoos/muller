# Agile

## Overview & Philosophy

Agile is an **umbrella philosophy** for software development (and increasingly other domains) that values adaptability, collaboration, and delivering working results frequently. It is not a specific methodology — it is a set of values and principles that inform concrete frameworks like Scrum, Kanban, and XP.

Agile originated from the **Agile Manifesto** (2001), written by 17 software practitioners at Snowbird, Utah.

**Core belief:** Responding to change and delivering working software frequently produces better outcomes than following a rigid plan.

## The Four Values (Agile Manifesto)

1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan

*"While there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more."*

## The Twelve Principles

1. Satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
3. Deliver working software frequently (weeks to months, prefer shorter).
4. Business people and developers work together daily.
5. Build projects around motivated individuals; give them support and trust.
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most effective communication.
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
8. Sustainable development pace, maintained indefinitely.
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design.
10. Simplicity — maximize the amount of work not done.
11. Self-organizing teams produce the best architectures, requirements, and designs.
12. Regular reflection and adjustment on how to become more effective.

## Work Structure

- Work is broken into **small, deliverable increments**.
- Exact structure depends on the implementing framework (e.g., user stories in Scrum, work items in Kanban).
- Emphasis on delivering a **working product increment** at regular intervals.
- Cross-functional teams own end-to-end delivery.

## Planning

- **Continuous and adaptive.** Planning happens at multiple horizons — release, iteration, daily.
- Requirements are discovered progressively, not all upfront.
- **Just-in-time planning:** detail is added as work items approach execution.
- Customer/stakeholder input is ongoing, not front-loaded.

## Cadence

- **Iterative.** Work is delivered in short cycles (typically 1–4 weeks).
- Specific cadence depends on framework (Scrum sprints, XP iterations, Kanban continuous flow).
- Regular inspect-and-adapt loops at every level.

## Scope Changes

- **Welcomed.** Agile Principle #2 explicitly embraces changing requirements.
- Scope is managed through a **prioritized backlog** that is continuously refined.
- Trade-off: new scope can enter; lower-priority items are deferred or dropped.
- No formal change control board — the product owner (or equivalent) reprioritizes.

## Blockers

- Surfaced quickly through daily communication (standups, board visibility).
- Team self-organizes to resolve; escalated to leadership only when necessary.
- Short iterations limit the blast radius of any single blocker.

## Reporting & Metrics

- **Working software** is the primary measure of progress.
- Common metrics: velocity, cycle time, lead time, burndown/burnup charts.
- Reporting tends to be lightweight and visual (boards, charts) rather than document-heavy.

## Ceremonies / Meetings

Agile itself does not prescribe specific ceremonies — those come from implementing frameworks. Common across Agile implementations:

- **Daily standup / sync** (short, focused)
- **Planning session** (per iteration or continuous)
- **Review / demo** (show working increment to stakeholders)
- **Retrospective** (reflect and improve the process)

## Roles

Agile does not prescribe specific roles. Common patterns across frameworks:

- **Product Owner / Customer representative:** prioritizes work, represents user needs.
- **Team members:** cross-functional, self-organizing.
- **Facilitator / Coach:** (Scrum Master, XP Coach, Agile Coach) removes impediments, fosters the process.

## Definition of Done

- Defined by the team (and sometimes the organization).
- Typically includes: coded, tested, integrated, reviewed, and potentially shippable.
- Specific DoD depends on the implementing framework.

## Best Suited For

- Projects with **evolving or unclear requirements**.
- Environments where **fast feedback** and course correction are valuable.
- Software development and knowledge work.
- Teams that can work cross-functionally and have access to stakeholders.

## Style-Specific: Agile as an Umbrella

Agile is a philosophy, not a process. When someone says they "do Agile," they typically mean they follow one or more **Agile frameworks** (Scrum, Kanban, XP, SAFe, Crystal, DSDM, FDD, etc.) that implement the Agile values and principles in concrete practices.
